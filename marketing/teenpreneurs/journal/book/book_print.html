The Teenpreneur Journal

If you’re expecting a book full of business buzzwords and textbook definitions, this isn’t it. And if I’m honest, that’s exactly the kind of book I would’ve ignored as a teenager.

Even though I have a master’s degree in economics, I didn’t write this journal to teach you about balance sheets or inflation. Why? Because I learned the most important things about life, entrepreneurship, and myself after university, not during it.

I created this book because I wish I had something like this when I was a teenager.

Back then, I didn’t fit the mould (I have ADHD, dyslexia, and the kind of brain that never quite sits still). I didn’t read books for fun, only when I absolutely had to pass an exam. And even then, I’d push it to the very last minute. Books didn’t speak to me… until I found the ones that finally did.

It wasn’t the official professors who changed my life. It was the unofficial ones: Steven Bartlett, Brené Brown, Stephen Covey, Napoleon Hill, Mel Robbins, Tony Robbins, Dean Graziosi, James Clear, and many more. They were the authors who handed me the mindset shifts, habits, and courage I needed to actually start building my life, not just pass tests.

I fell in love with books (audiobooks, to be precise) because suddenly learning felt like freedom, not a chore. It felt like answers to the questions I’d been carrying for years.

That’s why this journal exists. Not just to help you “start a business,” but to help you start something even bigger: your mindset, your confidence, your awareness, your voice.

This is for the dreamers and doers. The misunderstood and underestimated. The ones who might not feel “academic” but are absolutely brilliant in their own way.

Here’s to the crazy ones. The misfits. The rebels. The troublemakers. The round pegs in the square holes. The ones who see things differently. … They push the human race forward. And while some may see them as the crazy ones, we see genius. Because the people who are crazy enough to think they can change the world are the ones who do.

Hey,

If you’re reading this, I already know one thing about you: you’re not like everyone else. Not in a cheesy “you’re so unique” kind of way, but in a real, quiet way.

Maybe you’re curious. Maybe you question things more than your teachers or parents would like. Maybe you feel like something’s off with the way the world is set up (school, life, expectations, rules) but you can’t quite put your finger on what or why.

Or maybe you just want more. More meaning. More space. More truth.

This journal isn’t going to give you all the answers. But it might help you ask better questions. It might help you think bigger, go deeper, and notice how much power you already have, even if nobody’s ever told you that before.

It’s not about becoming a millionaire by 18. It’s not about getting perfect grades, or building a business, or impressing your parents. It’s about figuring out who you are, what actually matters to you, and how to live life in a way that feels honest, exciting, and yours.

You won’t find long to-do lists here. You will find questions. Challenges. Stories. Space to write, rant, plan, dream. And sometimes pages with nothing on them. On purpose. Because sometimes you just need space.

This isn’t a shortcut. It’s not a manual. It’s a mirror.

And if you use it, it will grow with you. Through wins, doubts, heartbreaks, ideas, boredom, and big, messy questions like:

  • What am I even doing with my life?
  • What if I don’t fit in?
  • What if I don’t want to?

So here’s the only rule: use this however you want.

You don’t have to go in order. You don’t have to finish it in a week or a year. You don’t have to impress anyone, including yourself. Just show up.

Write when it feels good. Think when something hits different. Ignore parts that feel off. You don’t have to answer every question. Start with the one that pulls you. And keep going.

Welcome to your real diary. The one you might just carry with you through your teens, and maybe a little longer.

PART 1

Wake up

The world is bigger than school.

Let’s be honest: school feels like a conveyor belt.

Everyone’s moving in the same direction, at the same speed, being told what to do, what to memorise, and when they’re allowed to ask questions.

It works for some people. Maybe even most.

But if you’ve ever felt like you’re meant for something different, something more, then you’ve probably already noticed that the system wasn’t really built for you.

Here’s the truth: much of the traditional system took shape in the industrial era, when the world needed people who could follow instructions in factories and offices. It was not designed for freedom, creativity, or entrepreneurship.

That’s why you’ve probably never been taught how to:

  • Deal with self-doubt
  • Create your own income
  • Understand how your mind actually works
  • Set real goals that mean something

Instead, you’re taught how to pass tests, follow instructions, and sit still for hours.

School also gives you real things: friends, teachers who care, and knowledge worth having. This journal is not against school. It adds what school rarely has time for.

Following the same path as everyone else doesn’t guarantee you’ll end up happy, or even successful.

If you’ve ever looked around and thought, “Is this it?”, you’re not alone. That question isn’t something to fear. It’s something to explore.

This chapter is about waking up to the fact that you don’t have to walk the beaten path. You can design your own. You can learn in your own way, think differently, and build a life that feels like yours, even if it looks nothing like the one they expected you to live.

But first, you need to start asking better questions.

Let’s clear something up: you’re not lazy.

You’re not broken. You’re not “just not motivated”. You’re probably just not being challenged in the right way.

If you’ve ever felt like you can focus for hours on the things that interest you, but shut down when you’re forced to do something pointless, that’s not a lack of discipline. It’s proof that you’re alive.

Real motivation doesn’t come from being pushed. It comes from being pulled, by things that spark your curiosity, your passion, your sense of purpose.

PART 2

Think differently

And bigger. Much bigger.

Let’s talk habits, but not in the boring way.

This isn’t about waking up at 5am, drinking green smoothies, or making colour-coded revision charts. It’s about becoming the kind of person who does the things that matter, without needing to force it every time.

Because here’s the truth: your future isn’t built by big, dramatic changes. It’s shaped by small actions you repeat every day. The way you speak to yourself. The way you respond to stress. The way you handle distractions, get started, or follow through.

And the most powerful part? You can rewire all of it. You don’t need to “fix” yourself. You just need to practise being the version of yourself you want to become, one small habit at a time.

It starts by asking a different question. Not “What do I want to achieve?” but “Who do I want to become?”

Because your habits follow your identity. If you see yourself as someone creative, consistent, or curious, you’ll naturally start doing things that match that belief. And if you don’t see yourself that way yet, you can start by proving it to yourself with small wins.

Even one tiny habit, done daily, can change how you see yourself. And when your identity shifts, your life does too.

So instead of waiting for motivation or the perfect time, just start small. Pick something that matters. Make it easy. And do it again tomorrow.

Have you ever felt like your thoughts take over, out of nowhere?

One moment you’re calm, the next you’re spiralling into stress, doubt or self-criticism. That’s not you being dramatic. That’s just how the teenage brain works, and you’re not alone.

Your mind is a tool. Not a dictator.

This chapter will show you how to recognise that tool, understand it, and start using it on your terms.

Your Teenage Brain

Right now, your brain is developing fast. The emotional part (the amygdala) matures earlier than the logical part (the prefrontal cortex). That means your feelings often react before your thinking can catch up.

Good news? Your brain is incredibly adaptable. Every time you practise something, like calming down or learning a new skill, your brain strengthens that connection. This is called neuroplasticity. It means your brain is always growing, and you can shape how it works.

Fixed vs Growth Mindset

A fixed mindset says: “I’m just not good at this.” A growth mindset says: “I’m not good at this YET.”

That one little word makes a huge difference. Believing you can improve, and acting on it, is how people grow in any area of life.

How to Manage Emotions

  1. Pause and breathe. Deep breathing helps calm your body and clear your head.
  2. Name the feeling. Saying “I feel nervous” helps your brain sort what’s happening.
  3. Don’t judge it. Feeling sad or angry isn’t wrong, it’s information.
  4. Express it. Write, talk, move, draw. Let it out in a way that doesn’t hurt you or others.

Dealing with Negative Self-Talk

Everyone has an inner voice. Sometimes it’s helpful. Sometimes it’s cruel. That negative voice is not the truth. It’s just loud. Try these steps:

  • Catch it: notice when you’re thinking something harsh.
  • Question it: is this thought 100% true?
  • Reframe it: “I failed” becomes “I learned something I can use next time.”
  • Talk to yourself like you’d talk to a good friend.

Thoughts Are Not Facts

Just because you think something doesn’t make it true.

You are not your thoughts. You are the one observing them. That means you can question them, redirect them, or even ignore them. Think of your thoughts like clouds. They pass through. You don’t have to hold onto every single one.

Unlock who you are

And who you could be.

You’re constantly told who to be. Good student. Polite child. Reliable friend. Future success story. But who are you, really?

If you don’t stop to ask that question, the world will answer it for you, and you might spend years chasing a life that isn’t even yours.

This chapter is all about getting honest with yourself. What do you care about? What kind of person do you want to be? And how do you build a life around that, instead of just trying to “fit in”?

Who Are You, Really?

Most people copy others without realising it. That’s normal. We’re wired to mirror the world around us. But following the crowd doesn’t always lead somewhere meaningful. If everyone is chasing the same grades, the same jobs, the same goals, but nobody’s asking why, it’s easy to lose yourself.

The question isn’t, “What should I do?” It’s, “What actually matters to me?”

Your Core Values

Your values are the things that matter most to you, not to your parents, teachers, friends or social media, but to you. When your actions align with your values, you feel grounded. When they don’t, you feel off.

Common values might include honesty, freedom, creativity, connection, kindness, learning, or challenge. There’s no right or wrong list, just what feels true to you.

How to Find Your Values

Think about moments when you felt proud, energised, or inspired. What were you doing? Who were you with? Why did it feel good? Now think about times you felt uncomfortable or frustrated. Was something important to you being ignored? Patterns will start to appear. That’s your real compass.

Create Your Own Definition of Success

The world will try to define success for you: fame, money, followers, diplomas. But real success feels different for everyone. For one person, it might be creating beautiful things. For another, helping others. For someone else, exploring the world.

Don’t copy someone else’s dream. Build your own.

Look around you. The people you spend time with shape your life more than you think. They influence how you think, how you talk, what you believe is possible, and even who you become.

So if you want to grow into the best version of yourself, you need to pay attention to your circle.

The Power of Influence

Humans are wired to fit in. We mirror the people around us to feel safe and accepted. That’s why your social circle is so powerful.

Hang around people who complain all the time, and you’ll start to see life as negative. Spend time with people who are curious, motivated and supportive, and you’ll feel more of those things too.

Audit Your Circle

Ask yourself: Who builds me up? Who brings me down? This doesn’t mean cutting people off harshly, but it does mean choosing where to invest your energy. It’s okay to outgrow relationships that no longer align with who you’re becoming.

How to Find Your People

You don’t need a big group. You just need a few real ones. Look for people who:

  • Get excited about learning or creating things.
  • Support you without jealousy or competition.
  • Make you feel safe to be yourself.
  • Encourage growth, not gossip.

Can’t find them nearby? Look online. Communities, courses, interest groups: your people exist, and they’re waiting too. Stay smart while you do: keep personal details private, and tell a trusted adult where you spend your time.

Create a Growth Bubble

If your current environment doesn’t support your goals, build your own. Fill your world with things and people that challenge and inspire you. Podcasts. Books. Mentors. Music. Coaches. Friends who want more from life.

Your surroundings can pull you down or lift you up. Choose carefully.

Do something real

Ideas are great. Action is everything.

Money isn’t just paper or numbers on a screen. It’s a tool for freedom, for choices, for change.

This chapter isn’t about becoming obsessed with money. It’s about understanding it, respecting it, and learning how to use it to create a life that feels free and fulfilling. Money can be part of your purpose. Let’s explore how.

What Money Really Is

Money is a way of exchanging value. That’s it. When you solve a problem, make a useful product, entertain someone, or help them in some way, and they’re willing to pay for it, that’s value. You don’t have to wait until you’re older to start thinking like someone who creates value.

Freedom Through Money

Money doesn’t make you happy. But it can give you options. It can help you travel, support people you love, say “no” to jobs you hate, or build something meaningful. It’s not about greed. It’s about choosing the kind of life you want, and having the means to live it.

Purpose-Driven Business

Some people think business is only about profit. But the best businesses solve real problems and make people’s lives better. You don’t have to be a “business person” to think like a problem-solver. Entrepreneurship is just about noticing problems and coming up with ideas to fix them, then testing, adjusting, and growing.

Business Ideas for Teens

Here are some ways teens are already making money while learning big skills:

  • Tutoring younger kids in subjects you’re good at
  • Making digital art, graphics or videos
  • Creating content around a passion (YouTube, TikTok, blogging)
  • Fixing or selling second-hand tech or clothes
  • Designing handmade jewellery or crafts
The key is to start small, learn as you go, and focus on helping others.

One practical thing: talk with a parent or carer before you sell anything or open an account in your name. The rules depend on your age and where you live.

Everyone wants to win. But the truth is, no one wins without first failing, a lot.

Behind every cool success story is someone who tested, messed up, learned, and kept going.

This chapter is about getting comfortable with the messy middle.

Why Failing Is Part of Winning

Failure isn’t the opposite of success. It’s part of it. Think of it like feedback. Every time something doesn’t work out, you learn something: what to do better, what not to do next time, what to try instead. Without trying and failing, you don’t grow. You just stay stuck.

Real Life Examples

  • J.K. Rowling was turned down by 12 publishers before Harry Potter was accepted.
  • Michael Jordan did not make the varsity basketball team in his second year of high school. He kept practising.
  • Elon Musk’s early SpaceX rockets exploded. Steve Jobs was pushed out of Apple, the company he started.
  • Decca Records turned down The Beatles in 1962, reportedly because guitar groups were “on the way out”.

They didn’t quit. They tested, failed, repeated, and kept moving.

The Myth of Perfection

Perfection is a trap. It makes you wait, overthink, and fear mistakes. Trying to be perfect will stop you from ever starting. But starting messy? That’s where the real progress happens.

You don’t need to get it right. You just need to get going.

Testing Your Ideas

Got a business idea? A passion project? A goal? Don’t wait until it’s perfect. Start small. Try it out. Get feedback. Adjust. Try again. That’s how real creators, entrepreneurs and artists do it. They test fast and learn faster.

Motivation feels amazing… until it disappears. One day you’re ready to change the world. The next, you can’t even change your bedsheets.

This chapter is about building systems that keep you moving forward, even when motivation doesn’t show up.

Why Motivation Fades

Motivation is like weather: it comes and goes. It’s based on emotions, energy levels, and even the time of day. Relying on it alone is risky. That’s why successful people build habits and systems to support their goals.

What Is a System?

A system is a routine or structure that helps you take action without needing to feel inspired. It’s a plan you follow even when you’re tired, bored or unsure. Like brushing your teeth, it becomes automatic over time.

Examples of Systems

  • A morning routine that sets you up for focus.
  • A checklist for getting homework done.
  • A weekly “check-in” where you plan and reflect.
  • An accountability buddy who helps you stay on track.

Building Your Own System

Start small. Choose one area of life you want to improve. Then think:

  • What tiny action could I repeat every day?
  • When will I do it?
  • What will remind me?

Track it. Celebrate it. Adjust it as needed. Systems should support you, not stress you out.

If a system keeps falling apart, the system is the problem, not you. Make it smaller, make it visible, make it fun.

Your brain is always listening. Watching. Absorbing. Everything you take in, the people you follow, the shows you binge, the music you play on repeat, shapes how you think, feel and act.

This chapter is about becoming the DJ of your own mind. If what you’re consuming isn’t helping you grow, you can change the track.

Input = Output

What you feed your mind is what your life will start to reflect.

Who Are You Following?

Digital Detox (Even a Mini One)

Fill your world with content that lifts you:

  • Podcasts that make you think
  • Books that open your perspective
  • Music that makes you feel strong
  • Voices that speak kindness, honesty, creativity

You are the gatekeeper of your mental space. Choose wisely.

Energy fuels everything: your thoughts, your focus, your mood, your motivation.

But it’s not just about getting enough sleep. It’s about knowing what drains you, what recharges you, and how to protect your energy like it matters, because it does.

Beyond Sleep

Yes, sleep is important. So is hydration, food, and movement. But energy isn’t just physical. It’s also emotional, mental, and social. Sometimes you’re tired not because you need rest, but because something (or someone) is draining your vibe.

Energy Boosters

Here are things that genuinely give most people more energy:

  • Being outside
  • Laughing with people you like
  • Moving your body (even just dancing or stretching)
  • Doing something creative or hands-on
  • Listening to music that shifts your mood
  • Helping someone else

Your list might look different. What matters is noticing what makes you feel alive.

Energy Thieves

These are different for everyone, but some common ones include:

  • Too much screen time (especially doom-scrolling)
  • Hanging out with people who complain or gossip
  • Doing things out of guilt, not purpose
  • Worrying about things you can’t control
  • Saying “yes” to everything and leaving no space for you

You don’t have to remove everything. Just notice where your energy leaks are.

Protect Your Energy

You don’t need to earn your rest. You’re allowed to recharge, no guilt. Set boundaries. Take breaks. Say no. Walk away from drama. Prioritise joy.

This isn’t selfish. It’s smart. Because when your energy is right, everything else flows better.

You’ve probably been told your whole life to follow the steps: Do well in school. Get good grades. Pick a career. Go to uni. Get a job. Buy a house. Retire.

But what if your path doesn’t look like that?

This chapter is about remembering that your future isn’t a checklist. It’s a story you get to write.

The Myth of the “One Path”

There is no single correct way to build a life. Some people thrive in university. Others build businesses, make music, travel, raise families, or invent things we haven’t even imagined yet.

The world is changing fast. Careers that don’t exist now might be common in 10 years. That means flexibility and self-awareness matter more than any fixed plan.

You Get to Decide

Your path should fit you, not the other way around. It doesn’t have to make sense to everyone else. You don’t need permission to take an unconventional route. Ask yourself: What do I want to explore? Who do I want to become? How do I want to grow? These questions are more powerful than any test score or title.

Redefining Success

Success isn’t one-size-fits-all. For some, it’s about impact. For others, creativity. For others, peace. Define your own version. Check in with it often. You’re allowed to change it as you grow.

Build a Life, Not Just a CV

AI tools can answer almost anything in seconds. That is exciting, and a little scary. The question for you isn’t whether to use AI. It’s how to use it so you get sharper, not lazier.

AI can be your sparring partner. It should never be your replacement.

Three things AI can’t do for you

  • Care about the problem. It doesn’t know what you notice, what bothers you, or what you want to change.
  • Choose. It can list ten options. You decide which one fits your values.
  • Be you. Your voice, your story and your questions are the whole point.

Five good ways to use it

  • Ask it to question your idea, not just praise it.
  • Ask it to explain something confusing, in simple words.
  • Ask for feedback on a draft you wrote yourself.
  • Brainstorm 20 bad ideas, then pick the least bad one and improve it.
  • Role play a customer, a teacher or a sceptical friend, so you can practise.

Four rules to keep

  1. Think first. Write your own first attempt, then bring in the AI.
  2. Check the facts. AI can sound completely sure and be completely wrong.
  3. Protect your privacy. Never share your full name, address, school, or photos of other people.
  4. Be honest. Say when AI helped you. And check the age rules for any tool, and talk with a parent or carer about how you use it.

The “explain it” test

Close the chat. Can you explain your idea to a friend in your own words? If not, you haven’t learned it yet.

The Voice in Your Head (You’re Not Alone)

So doubt showed up again, huh? That annoying little voice is whispering, “You’re not good enough. You’re a fraud. Why even try?” First off, breathe. There’s nothing wrong with you. Nearly everyone hears a nagging voice like that sometimes, even your super confident friend, even your favourite celebrity.

It even has a name: imposter syndrome, the feeling that you’re not as capable as other people think. Estimates of how common it is vary a lot, but it is very common. Michelle Obama has said she still feels it. Self-doubt is part of being human, and you’re in good company. The difference-maker is what you do when doubt sneaks in.

Successful people aren’t successful because they never have self-doubt. They’re successful because they move forward despite it.

Why Doubt Can Be a Good Sign

Here’s a plot twist: that knot in your stomach and the voice shouting “Who do you think you are?” might actually be good news. You usually feel imposter syndrome exactly when you’re stretching beyond your comfort zone, trying something new, bold, or important to you. You don’t get it from sitting on the couch doing nothing. You get it when you’re reaching higher.

Doubt often means you actually care about what you’re doing. Next time you feel those jitters, try telling yourself: “This feeling means I’m growing. My brain is just catching up with my new reality.”

If you’re feeling a bit of fear, maybe, just maybe, there’s a gift waiting for you on the other side of it.

Meet Your Inner Villain (and Your Inner Hero)

Let’s talk about that voice inside, the harsh one that loves to point out your flaws. Call it the inner critic, or the inner villain. You don’t have to accept everything it says as truth. It’s only one part of your mind. You also have an inner hero, a kinder, braver voice that celebrates your strengths and dreams. When we’re stressed or scared, the villain tends to shout louder. Our job is to turn up the volume on the hero instead.

Journal exercise

Give your inner critic a fun (or better, ridiculous) name. “Doubting Dora.” “Negative Nigel.” Something that makes you smirk. Now give your encouraging voice a name too, like “Heroine Hannah” or “Brave Ben”. Next time Dora starts ranting that you’re not enough, you can say, “Ugh, here goes Dora again,” and invite Hannah to speak up.

Flip the Script: Tools to Tackle Doubt

Pause & breathe

When anxiety or imposter feelings flood in, pause. Take a deep breath in, hold for 5 seconds, then exhale slowly. Do this a few times. It sounds basic, but it works. Then acknowledge the doubt without judgment: “I see you, fear. You’re trying to protect me, but I’m still in charge.”

Don’t believe everything you think

Your mind spits out thousands of thoughts a day, and not all of them are true or useful. Treat doubtful thoughts like passing clouds, not facts carved in stone. Ask: “What evidence do I have for this?” Question it, shrink it, rewrite it. A thought is just a thought, not a prophecy.

Flip the script

Replace “I can’t do this” with “I can try, and that’s enough for now.” Replace “I’m not as good as them” with “I’m learning, and every expert was once a beginner.” This isn’t fake positivity. It’s seeing the whole picture. Write down a mantra that fits you, like “I’ve got this, one step at a time,” and keep it where you can see it.

Remember your why

When fear kicks in, reconnect with your purpose. Why did you want to do this in the first place? Purpose fuels courage. Finish this sentence in your journal: “I want to do ___ because ___.” Make the second blank something that fires you up, not “because I have to”.

Tiny action, big wins

The longer you sit paralysed by doubt, the stronger it grows. So do one teeny step. Open a new document and write one sentence. Send one text. Say hi once. Or use the rocket launch: count down 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 and go, before your mind talks you out of it. Action first, confidence second.

Show perfectionism the door

A lot of doubt comes from thinking you need to be perfect. Perfection is not only impossible, it’s the enemy of progress. Aim for learning, not perfecting. Write this somewhere: progress over perfection. Done is better than perfect. Each “failure” is just an event, not a definition of you.

Phone a friend

Don’t isolate yourself. Talk to someone you trust: a friend, sibling, parent, mentor. Telling someone “Honestly, I’m really doubting myself” can be a relief, and a true friend will remind you of your strengths when you can’t see them. As Brené Brown teaches, vulnerability isn’t weakness. It’s courage. Even superheroes have sidekicks.

Talk to yourself like you would to a younger sibling

We’re often our own worst critics. But if your little brother, sister or best friend said, “I feel like a total failure,” you wouldn’t agree and tell them to give up. You’d encourage them. You deserve the same kindness. Try: “Okay, I’m feeling like a fraud, but hey, so does everyone sometimes.”

Quick Confidence Boosters

Power pose

Stand up, roll your shoulders back, hands on hips like a superhero. Chin up. Take up space. Hold for 30 seconds. Some people find it helps them feel braver. Try it and see.

Music therapy

Blast your favourite pump-up song for a minute or two.

Visualise victory

Close your eyes and imagine yourself after you’ve pushed through the doubt and succeeded. Let that future pride fuel your present action.

Affirmation attack

Write an empowering statement on a sticky note and stick it where you’ll see it every day.

Your Challenge: Turning Doubt into Dominance

You’ve got the knowledge. You’ve got the tools. Now it’s go time. When the doubt comes (and it will), you have a choice. You can feed the inner villain, or you can fuel your inner hero. You hold the reins, not your fear.

The challenge: do one brave thing in the next 24 hours. It doesn’t have to be huge or perfect. Just one thing that nudges you out of your comfort zone despite the self-doubt. The smaller and more immediate, the better.

Some ideas: speak up about an idea you’ve been quiet about. Apply for that opportunity you’ve been avoiding. Start a conversation with someone you’d like to know better. Try a skill that intrigues you, even if you might be terrible at first.

Then journal about it. What did you do? How did you feel before, during, after? What did you learn about yourself? Even if it was awkward, you’re still here, still breathing, and now a little bit stronger and wiser. That is success.
